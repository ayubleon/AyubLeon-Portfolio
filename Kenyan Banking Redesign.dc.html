Kenyan Banking Redesign

Overhauling digital banking friction across four major regional apps. Self-initiated, 2025.

Role

UX Designer, UI Designer

Type

Self-initiated

Timeline

2025

Platforms

Mobile

Overview

I'm a frustrated customer of four banking apps in Kenya: DTB, Co-op Bank's Omni, NCBA Loop, and I&M, and this project started from that frustration.

None of the four felt straightforward. One buried features, the kind that actually drive revenue for the bank, in corners of the app where they were hard to find or easy to miss entirely. Another was missing basic functionality I needed for everyday banking. Across all of them, the interface felt unfinished, and a well-designed interface isn't just cosmetic, it's one of the things that makes people trust an app enough to actually use it for their money.

A frustrated user doesn't file a complaint, they just use the app less or switch banks.

My role

I owned this project end to end, research, navigation, and interface design, working alone as a self-initiated effort. In response to what I found, I focused on two things: fixing the navigation so features were easy to find and land on, and fixing the interface itself, since a broken-looking app undermines trust before a user even gets to the features. Organization and access were the priority, not just a visual refresh.

The advantage

The advantage that made it work

I wasn't designing for a hypothetical user, I was the user. Every pain point in this case study is one I'd actually run into trying to do my own banking, and when I supplemented that with feedback from friends who'd hit the same walls, I wasn't guessing at problems, I already knew where to look.

Challenges

Navigating challenges & quality control

Validating without a live product

Since this wasn't a client engagement, I didn't have a real user base to test against. I shared the prototype with friends who work in frontend development and with people who'd specifically complained about their own banking apps, closer to informal peer feedback than a formal usability study, but the response consistently pointed to the same thing: the reorganized navigation and easier-to-find features landed well.

Impact

Business & workflow impact

This hasn't been picked up by a bank yet, and I haven't formally pitched it to one. But I've started using it directly in outreach to Kenyan banks, as a concrete example of the value I could bring rather than just a portfolio piece. It's a passion project, and I'd like nothing more than to turn it into something real for the millions of people who use these apps every day.

What's shown here is the first working draft; the app went through several rounds of revision after it. The full write-up of how I got there is on Behance.
